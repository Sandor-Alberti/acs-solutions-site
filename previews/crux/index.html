When something changes.

  1. 1

    Flag it

    Anyone who spots a wrong or out-of-date answer can flag it.

  2. 2

    Someone owns it

    A manager reviews the flag and can give the fix an owner and a due date.

  3. 3

    Fix it in the open

    The correction sits next to the original answer. Nothing is quietly rewritten.

  4. 4

    Everyone gets the update

    New answers use the updated guide. Older answers still show what they were based on.

A question Crux can’t answer becomes a gap with an owner. A manager closes it by linking an approved source.

Before you begin.

What do we need to connect?

Start with written knowledge or conversation transcripts you can upload or paste. Markdown and TXT work for step-by-step instructions, handbooks, decision notes and role handoffs; VTT and SRT keep transcript timestamps. Files are added by hand. PDF, DOCX, and connected Drive or Slack imports are not supported.

Who can see the knowledge and questions?

A workspace serves one company and its invited members. Approved sources are available to everyone in that workspace; drafts are visible to managers. Managers can review employee questions and answers, so the chat is not private from your company.

What if an answer is wrong?

Anyone can flag an answer. A manager can review it or add a correction beside the original. An updated, approved source makes the correction available to future answers. Citations make the evidence easier to check; they do not guarantee an answer is correct.

How should we handle sensitive material?

Only add information your company is allowed to share with everyone in the workspace. Basic automated redaction can miss personal and confidential details, so a manager must review the content. Agree on hosting, retention and model-provider requirements before using sensitive material.
